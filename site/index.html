Does trading

A faster portfolio can catch a new trend. It can also trade away its advantage. We test what changes when momentum strategies rebalance more often.

The short answerField notes / 01

Sometimes.
Not consistently.

Across these two studies, the answer changed with the period, trading costs, and the portfolio being tested. Neither produced a universal case for trading faster.

01

The year mattered. Monthly stock selection led in 2026 YTD after trailing in 2025.

02

Costs mattered. The stock pilot’s small full-period advantage disappeared at higher trading costs.

03

Risk mattered too. Monthly sector-ETF selection had a shallower drawdown and lower long-period growth.

Historical simulations, with model and data limitations. No forecast or universal winner.

How we ask the question

Change the timing.
Show the trade-offs.

Momentum strategies favor assets with strong past performance. Our central question is how often to update that selection.

01

Keep the comparison controlled.

Within each primary pair, keep the universe, signal and portfolio rules fixed. Change monthly versus semiannual selection.

02

Charge for the extra activity.

Model costs on every buy and sell. Show 0, 5, 10 and 25 bps per side, alongside the return and drawdown.

03

Keep the inconvenient results.

Publish different periods, calendar schedules and signal variants. A favorable year never replaces the full record.
